How to use the solver well

The tool is most useful when you supply a clear constraint and then verify unfamiliar results.

Step 1

Enter the rack

Type letters only. Keep a blank as ? or *. Example: CART? contains four fixed letters and one flexible tile.

Step 2

Set the length

Use an exact length for a full anagram. Use a range when looking for legal plays of several sizes.

Step 3

Check the context

Confirm rare results in the dictionary required by your game, class, or publication.

Worked example: AELRST

Sort the rack as A-E-L-R-S-T. A subset search finds three-letter foundations such as ART, RAT, SEA, and SET. Four-letter extensions include RATE, STAR, and TALE. Five-letter candidates include ALERT, ALTER, LATER, STALE, STEAL, SLATE, and TALES. Each candidate is accepted only when its letter counts fit the rack.

Exact mode: Exact mode keeps only six-letter candidates using every supplied letter. Subset mode returns shorter buildable words as well.

Algorithm in plain English

The solver counts every letter in the rack and every letter in a candidate. If a candidate asks for more copies than the rack contains, it is rejected unless a blank covers the difference. It then sorts accepted words by length, estimated tile value, and alphabetic order.

Limits and dictionary scope

This version intentionally uses a curated common-English list rather than claiming every obscure token on the web is a valid word. It is useful for everyday puzzles and demonstrations, but official games use specific word lists. Tournament players should verify unfamiliar entries in the named official list.

Common questions

Does the tool send my letters anywhere?

No. Matching happens in your browser.

Why is a word missing?

It may be outside our curated list or excluded by your length settings. The absence does not prove the word is invalid.

Does a blank score points?

Game rules commonly score a blank as zero, even though it represents a letter. Our simple displayed score does not subtract for blank use, so calculate the official move separately.

How the guides in this section are written

Every guide in the AI section of HaoWordTool starts from a question a real buyer or builder actually asked, and it has to answer that question with something checkable: a calculation you can redo yourself, a comparison dimension you can verify on a product page, or a workflow you can follow step by step. If a page cannot do that, it does not belong in this index. That is why this section is deliberately small. We would rather publish twelve guides that hold up than two hundred pages of paraphrased product descriptions, and the index is pruned whenever a page stops earning its place.

The writing process is the same each time. First we collect the raw material: manufacturer specifications, marketplace listings, reader questions, and our own test notes where a product is physically on hand. Then we separate facts from opinions. Facts — battery capacities, fabric compositions, subscription prices, file format limits — are stated plainly and dated, because they change. Opinions are labelled as opinions and always come with the reasoning, so you can disagree productively instead of having to trust a score. You will not find star ratings or invented verdicts in these guides, because a score we cannot back with a documented test would be decoration, not information.

The heated shawl research series

The most complete cluster here grew out of one winter product: the USB heated shawl. Instead of a single review, the series breaks the buying decision into the four questions that actually determine whether owners keep using it. The buying guide compares construction, heating zones and controls across the market. The power bank guide works through the arithmetic of runtime — milliamp-hours, conversion losses, and why a 10,000 mAh power bank does not deliver 10,000 mAh of heat. The price breakdown explains what the step up in price actually buys in materials and safety features, and what it does not. The troubleshooting guide covers the most common disappointment, a shawl that feels lukewarm, which is usually a USB power delivery problem rather than a broken product. Read in order, the four pages will tell you whether the product category fits your life before you spend anything.

AI money cases and the boards

The second cluster documents how independent builders are making money with AI products. The case library breaks down thirty products in a fixed format: what it sells, who pays, how it grew, and the three steps a solo builder could realistically copy. The boards rank AI money-making sites and products by category, with a strict rule that income figures are always labelled as reported and self-declared — we show the numbers founders publish, and we tell you to discount them, because nobody audits a landing page. The market notes go deeper on one topic at a time, such as the economics of GPU compute resale or what the optical-fibre bubble teaches about AI infrastructure spending. This material is research, not financial advice, and it is written for builders looking for a model to adapt, not for investors looking for a tip.

Tools, not just reading

Several pages in this section are working tools rather than articles. Social Media Extract turns public social video links into transcripts, view data and downloadable material you can file into a notes system. The AI search visibility material pairs its explanation with a checklist you can run against your own site. Where a tool is paid, the pricing is on the page before you sign up, and where a workflow depends on third-party platforms we say so, including what breaks when those platforms change their rules.

How to choose which guide to read first

If you arrived with a product in your cart, start with the buying guide for that category and the price breakdown; they are written to be read before money moves. If you already own the product and something feels off, the troubleshooting guide will save you a return. If you are here to build rather than buy, begin with one money case in a category you understand, then open the matching board and read its entries against the case — the case shows the depth, the board shows the field, and the market notes explain the weather both are operating in.

Corrections and updates

Prices, specifications and platform rules drift, so each guide carries its context in the text and older pages are revisited when their facts expire. If you find an error — a wrong capacity figure, a discontinued model, a link that no longer resolves — the contact page reaches a human, and corrections are made in the open with the guide updated rather than silently replaced. That standard is slower than publishing at volume. It is also the only standard that makes an index like this worth your click.

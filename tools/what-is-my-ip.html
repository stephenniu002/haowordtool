What an IP address reveals, and what it does not

Your public address is the one the internet sees, which is normally your router's address rather than your device's. It tells a server roughly where you are at city level, because allocation is regional — but it does not give your street, your name, or your precise location. City-level geolocation is right most of the time and wrong often enough that it should never be treated as evidence of where someone is.

IPv4, IPv6 and why you may see two

Most connections now have both. IPv4 addresses are exhausted and shared through carrier-grade NAT, so several households can present the same address; IPv6 gives each device its own. A site that only speaks IPv4 will see your IPv4 address, and the two can map to different cities in geolocation databases, which is why results sometimes look inconsistent between tools.

A VPN or proxy changes what this page reports, because it sees the exit node rather than your connection. That is the whole point of one, and it also means the "location" shown is the exit node's.

Questions that come up

Is my IP stored? No. The lookup is made from your browser to public APIs and nothing is logged here.

Can I hide it? Only by routing through something else — a VPN, a proxy, or Tor.

Why does it change? Home connections usually hold an address for hours or days; mobile networks rotate far more often.

Why is the location wrong on mobile data? Mobile carriers route traffic through regional gateways, so the address can be registered to a city hundreds of kilometres from where you are standing.

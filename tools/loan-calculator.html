Worked example

A 250,000 loan at 5.5% over 25 years costs 1,535.05 a month. Over the term you pay 460,515 — of which 210,515 is interest, roughly 84% of the amount borrowed.

Amortisation means early payments are mostly interest and late payments are mostly principal. The monthly figure is constant; the split changes every month, which is why paying a little extra at the start saves disproportionately more than the same extra payment at the end.

What this calculator assumes

A fixed rate for the entire term, no fees, no overpayments, and interest calculated monthly. Variable-rate loans, arrangement fees and early-repayment charges all change the real cost, sometimes by more than the rate difference.

What the monthly figure leaves out

The payment is principal and interest only. Insurance, arrangement fees, stamp duty and any monthly service charge sit outside it, and on a long mortgage they can add a meaningful percentage to the real cost of borrowing. Compare offers on the annual percentage rate rather than the headline interest rate, because the APR is required to fold in the fees.

Term length is the lever with the largest effect: stretching the repayment lowers the monthly figure and raises the total interest, often by more than intuition suggests. Check whether the rate is fixed, variable or fixed for an introductory period — the payment shown is only stable in the first case.

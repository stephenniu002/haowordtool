Your age

How exact age is counted

Age in years alone hides the interesting part. The years-months-days form is a subtraction with borrows: when the current day is smaller than the birth day, you borrow the length of the previous month, which is why the answer differs by a day or two depending on which months you passed through. Someone born on the 31st has no corresponding day in a 30-day month, and the usual convention is to treat the end of that month as the anniversary.

Leap days add a second wrinkle. A 29 February birthday exists only in leap years in strict counting, while most legal systems treat 1 March as the anniversary in common years. The calculator follows the arithmetic, not the local statute — for anything official, the registry's convention governs.

Why the time zone matters

Age is computed from calendar dates, but "today" depends on where you are. Near midnight, a device in one zone and a form processed in another can disagree by a day. For deadlines and eligibility cut-offs, the date that counts is normally the one in the jurisdiction that set the rule.

Questions that come up

Does it count the birth day itself? No — age is zero on the day you are born.

Can it show total days lived? The same subtraction expressed in days is available, and it is what people usually want for milestone counters.

Why is my next birthday a day off? Almost always the 29 February or month-end convention described above.

Can it count down to a future date? The same subtraction works in either direction; set the date ahead and the result reads as time remaining rather than time elapsed.

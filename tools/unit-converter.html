Two kinds of conversion

Most unit conversions are a single multiplication: inches to centimetres is a fixed factor, and so is pounds to kilograms. Temperature is not. Celsius and Fahrenheit have different zero points as well as different step sizes, so the conversion is a linear function with an offset — multiply and add, in that order. Applying a factor alone, which is the usual mistake, gives plausible-looking nonsense for anything well below freezing.

Where precision quietly disappears

Exact definitions exist for most imperial units: an inch is defined as 2.54 centimetres, so those conversions are exact to any number of digits. Others are conventions with limited precision, and rounding at each step of a chain of conversions accumulates error. Convert once, from the original value, rather than converting a rounded intermediate result.

Watch the units that share a name. A US gallon is about 3.79 litres and an imperial gallon about 4.55 — a 20% difference that has sunk real projects. A US ton is 2,000 pounds, a metric tonne 2,204.6, and a UK ton historically 2,240.

Questions that come up

How many digits should I keep? Match the precision of the input; extra digits imply accuracy that is not there.

Is the conversion done locally? Yes, in the browser, with no request to a server.

Why do two converters disagree slightly? Different rounding, or different definitions of the same unit name.

Can it convert area or volume? Those need squared and cubed factors, which is why they are kept separate rather than folded into the length converter; a square metre is not 100 times a square centimetre but 10,000.

Why does the temperature result look odd? Because it is the one conversion on the page that is not a pure multiplication, and rounding the intermediate step before adding the offset shifts the answer.

What the rate on this page is

The rates here are a fixed snapshot bundled with the page, not a live feed. That is a deliberate trade: the tool works offline, loads instantly, and sends no request to a third party, but the numbers do not move with the market. For budgeting, travel planning and rough comparisons that is fine. For anything that settles — invoicing, payments, contracts — use the rate your bank or payment provider will actually apply, because that rate includes their spread.

Why the rate you get is worse than the rate you see

Mid-market rates are what currencies trade at between banks, in enormous size. Retail conversion adds a margin, sometimes as a hidden difference between the buy and sell price rather than a visible fee. A card that advertises "no fees" typically earns its money on the spread, and the gap is often one to three percent — far more than a visible commission would be.

Also mind the direction. A rate quoted as units of foreign currency per unit of domestic is not symmetric with its reciprocal; multiplying by the wrong one gives an answer that is wrong by the square of the rate and looks plausible for currencies near parity.

Questions that come up

How often are the rates updated? They are not — the table is static.

Why does my bank show a different number? Spread, plus the timestamp of their rate.

Is the conversion exact? It is exact arithmetic on approximate inputs; treat three significant figures as honest.

What if my currency is not listed? Convert through a major currency in two steps. The result carries the error of both rates, so treat it as an estimate rather than a quote.

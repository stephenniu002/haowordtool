Worked example

At 175 cm, the three male formulas give 65.2, 65.4 and 65.5 kg — close together here, but they diverge by several kilograms at the extremes of height.

These formulas were built for drug dosing, not for setting personal targets. They assume a specific body composition and were derived from mid-twentieth-century insurance data, which is why three of them disagree by a few kilograms on the same person.

Use the range

The healthy BMI band for your height is a wider and more defensible target than any single formula. If your weight sits inside it and your measurements are stable, the precise "ideal" number adds nothing.

Why the answer is a range

Ideal weight formulas were built for insurance tables and drug dosing, not for judging a body. They differ from each other by several kilograms at the same height, which is why this page reports a range rather than a single target. The band corresponds to a BMI of roughly 18.5 to 24.9, the range where mortality data is flattest.

Two limits matter. The formulas were derived from mid-century populations and are least reliable at the extremes of height. And none of them can tell muscle from fat: a trained athlete can sit above the range with a low body fat percentage, and a sedentary person can sit inside it with a high one. Treat the number as a reference point, not a goal.

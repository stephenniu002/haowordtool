What the Word Counter Counts

Paste or type text and the tool reports words, characters (with and without spaces), sentences, paragraphs and estimated reading time. Everything updates as you type — there is no submit button and no processing delay, because the counting happens locally in your browser.

Nothing you paste is transmitted anywhere. That matters more than it sounds: people use word counters for drafts, private notes, contracts and application essays, and a tool that uploads text to a server would be the wrong design for all of them.

How to Use It

Step 1 — Paste your text into the input area, or start typing directly.

Step 2 — Read the counts in the panel. They update on every keystroke.

Step 3 — Check the limit you are targeting and trim until the number fits.

Step 4 — Copy the text out with the copy button, or clear and start again.

Why Exact Counts Matter

Search meta descriptions

Google truncates meta descriptions by pixel width rather than character count, but as a working rule 150 to 160 characters is the safe band. Go past it and the tail is cut mid-sentence, which looks careless in search results and lowers click-through.

Title tags

Title tags get roughly 60 characters before truncation on desktop. Front-load the keyword and the distinguishing detail, because the end is what disappears.

Social platform limits

Post limits vary by platform and change without notice, which makes a live counter more reliable than a memorised number. The character count is the one that matters for these, not the word count.

Academic word limits

Essays, abstracts and applications are usually hard-capped, and going over is often an automatic penalty rather than a small deduction. Counting before submitting is cheaper than finding out afterwards.

Translation and freelance pricing

Translation is normally quoted per source word. Knowing the exact count before you request a quote is the difference between a firm price and an awkward renegotiation.

Reading time and content planning

Editors budget articles in words because words convert predictably into reading minutes. A 1,000-word draft and a 2,500-word draft are different products, not different lengths of the same product.

Word Count vs Character Count vs Byte Count

These three get confused constantly, and the confusion causes real problems.

Words are runs of text separated by whitespace. Hyphenated compounds usually count as one word. This is the number publishers and translation agencies use.

Characters are individual glyphs. Spaces may or may not be included — a meaningful distinction, since a 200-character limit that includes spaces is roughly 30 characters tighter than one that does not.

Bytes are a storage measure, and they are not the same as characters. In UTF-8, an English letter is one byte, but an accented character, an emoji or a CJK ideograph takes two to four bytes. If a database column or an SMS segment limits you in bytes, counting characters will overestimate how much room you have.

Mixed-language text is where this bites hardest. A paragraph of Chinese that looks short can exceed a byte limit that a much longer English paragraph passes comfortably.

Reading Time and Speaking Time

The estimated reading time uses an average adult silent reading speed of roughly 200 to 250 words per minute. That is a population average, not a prediction for your specific audience: technical material with formulas, unusual terminology or heavy cross-referencing reads much slower, while a list of short familiar items reads faster.

Spoken delivery is slower and more variable — around 130 to 150 words per minute for a presentation, less if you pause, gesture or take questions. If you are writing a script for a talk or a video, budget by spoken rate rather than reading rate, or you will overrun every time.

Tips for Hitting a Target Length

  • Cut whole sentences, not words. Trimming adjectives leaves the structure intact but the argument thin. Removing a redundant sentence usually improves the piece and drops twenty words at once.
  • Kill throat-clearing openers. Phrases like "It is important to note that" and "In today's fast-paced world" consume characters without adding meaning.
  • Count before you polish. If you are 200 words over the limit, you need structural cuts. Discovering that after line-editing wastes the editing.
  • Watch em dashes and smart quotes. Word processors sometimes substitute these automatically, which changes the byte count while leaving the word count untouched.

FAQ

Does the word counter count hyphenated words as one word?

Yes. A hyphenated compound such as well-known is counted as a single word because there is no whitespace inside it. This matches how most publishers and translation agencies count.

How is the reading time calculated?

The estimate divides the word count by roughly 200 to 250 words per minute, which is a typical adult silent reading speed. Dense technical text reads slower and simple lists read faster, so treat it as an approximation.

Is my text uploaded to a server?

No. All counting happens in your browser using local JavaScript. The text you paste never leaves your device.

Can I count characters without spaces?

Yes. The panel reports both character counts, with and without spaces, since different limits refer to different numbers.

Does it work for languages without spaces between words?

The word count relies on whitespace separation, so for Chinese, Japanese and Korean text the word number will be unreliable. Use the character count instead, which is what those platforms and publishers actually measure.

Is there a limit on how much text I can paste?

There is no imposed limit, but extremely large pastes are bounded by your browser's available memory. Tens of thousands of words work without issue.

Is the tool free?

Yes, free and without sign-up. There is no account, no paywall and no export restriction.

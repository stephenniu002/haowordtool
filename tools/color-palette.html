Worked example

The percentages are shares of sampled pixels, not of visual importance. A large area of sky will dominate the list even if the photograph is about the subject.

Palette extraction counts pixels in colour buckets and returns the largest. That is why the result often looks duller than the image feels: the dominant colour of a landscape photograph is usually sky or foliage, not the thing you noticed.

Getting a useful palette

Crop to the region whose colour you want before uploading. A tight crop of a subject gives a palette about that subject; the full frame gives a palette about the scene.

Reading the result sensibly

The extractor groups pixels by similarity and returns the most populated groups, which means the count you ask for changes the answer: a small number returns broad averages, a large number starts returning near-duplicates that differ by a few units. Five to eight is usually the useful range for a design palette.

Photographs produce muddy palettes because natural images contain thousands of intermediate tones — a sky alone contributes dozens of blues. Flat graphics, logos and UI screenshots give cleaner results. If you need a palette from a photo, crop to the subject first so the background stops voting.

Everything runs in the browser; the image is never uploaded, which also means very large files are limited by the memory of the tab rather than by a server limit.

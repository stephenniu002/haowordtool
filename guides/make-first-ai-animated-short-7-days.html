How to Make Your First AI Animated Short in 7 Days (Beginner Roadmap)

A day-by-day 7-day roadmap for beginners: go from zero to a finished 30-second AI animated short with one focused hour per day. Concept, script, characters, scenes, motion, editing.

By HaoWord Studio Editorial · 6 min · Updated 2026-09-25

"Learn AI animation" without a schedule quietly turns into three months of watching tutorials and producing nothing. The fix is a constrained, day-by-day plan: one focused hour per day, one concrete deliverable per day, and a finished 30-second animated short by day seven.

This roadmap assumes you already have your five-tool stack picked (script, image, video, voice, editing — see our toolkit guide). If not, do that first; it takes one sitting. Then block seven one-hour sessions on your calendar and follow the plan below.

Day 1 — Concept (1 Hour)

Pick your genre and your main character. Write one sentence for each. "A mystery series about a retired detective who is secretly a house cat." "A comedy about a nervous robot chef who takes everything literally."

Do not aim for originality yet; aim for clarity. A simple concept executed well beats a brilliant concept stuck in your head. When the hour ends, you should be able to pitch your short in ten seconds.

Day 2 — Script (1 Hour)

Use your script-writing LLM to draft a 30-second script. Thirty seconds of screen time is roughly 75 words of dialogue — less than you think. Structure it as hook (0–3 seconds), two to three beats, and a payoff twist or joke.

Prompt the model with your constraints up front: genre, character, length, and the payoff you want. Then do the read-aloud test: read the draft out loud with a timer running. AI almost always overwrites, so cut about 20 percent. In animation, the visuals carry meaning — dialogue should tell as little as possible.

Day 3 — Character Design (1 Hour)

Generate your main character's reference sheet: a front view, a side view, and two or three expression variants. Use one locked character description pasted verbatim into every prompt — appearance, clothing, art style, all of it.

This sheet is the single highest-leverage artifact of your first week. Every image you generate from day 4 onward gets compared against it. Consistency across shots is what separates "AI slideshow" from "animated short," and it starts here.

Day 4 — Scenes (1 Hour)

Generate your backgrounds — one per shot. For a 30-second short, three shots is the sweet spot for week one: an establishing wide shot, a medium shot for the main action, and a close-up for the payoff moment.

Include your style keywords in every scene prompt so the backgrounds match your character art. Save each approved background in a folder named for the project; you will animate them tomorrow.

Day 5 — Voices and Motion (1 Hour)

Split the hour in two. First thirty minutes: generate your dialogue audio with your AI voice tool, one distinct voice per character. Listen back with headphones — bad audio is the fastest way to lose a viewer, so re-generate any line that sounds flat or mispronounced.

Second thirty minutes: animate your three shots with your image-to-video tool. Keep motions simple and short — a slow push-in, a head turn, a blink. Three-to-six-second clips are all you need; the edit will create the energy.

Day 6 — Assembly (1 Hour)

Import everything into your editor: three animated clips, dialogue audio, and then layer on royalty-free music, two or three sound effects (a door creak, a comedic boing, a whoosh), and burned-in subtitles.

Subtitles deserve emphasis: most short-video viewing happens with sound off at first, and captions are what convert a scroller into a watcher. Keep them to a few words per card, synced tightly to the dialogue.

Export at 1080×1920 for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Watch the export once all the way through before calling it done.

Day 7 — Private Review (1 Hour)

Upload the short as unlisted or private. Watch it twice — once as a creator checking for glitches, once pretending you have never seen it. Then write down exactly three things to improve next time. Not ten. Three.

This review ritual is the compounding mechanism of the whole roadmap. Each week's three improvements stack, and after a month you will barely recognize your day-7 output.

Two Habits That Make the Roadmap Work

The daily shutdown ritual. At the end of each hour, do three things: save every file in a folder named for the day, write one sentence ("Today I finished ___"), and write tomorrow's first concrete step. Hypothetically, imagine a beginner who skips this and spends day 4 re-generating day 3's character because she cannot remember which version she approved. In this illustration, ten minutes of organizing saves an hour of rework — every single day.

Batch your AI generations. Queue all image generations in one sitting and do something else while they render. AI tools queue; your attention should not. Generate in batches, review in batches, approve in batches.

Summary

One hour a day for seven days — concept, script, character sheet, scenes, voices plus motion, assembly, review — takes you from zero to a finished 30-second AI animated short. The roadmap works because each day has exactly one deliverable, and the daily shutdown ritual plus batched generation protect your momentum.

FAQ

I have never made any video before. Is 7 days realistic?

Yes, for a simple 30-second short, because the scope is deliberately tiny: one character, three shots, 75 words of dialogue. The roadmap is designed so each day's task fits in an hour even if every tool is new to you.

What if I fall behind a day?

Do not double up — just shift the schedule. The enemy is not slowness, it is the guilt spiral that makes you quit. One hour, one deliverable, whenever you can do it.

Should my first short be public?

Keep day 7 private. Your first short is a learning artifact, not a launch. Publish publicly once you have completed the roadmap two or three times and your review notes start repeating "looks good."

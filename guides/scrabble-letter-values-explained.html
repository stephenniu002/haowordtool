Scrabble Letter Values: Score, Scarcity, and Opportunity

Why a high-value tile is not always valuable, and how board multipliers change the calculation.

Editorial guide · 8 min · Updated August 28, 2026

Face value is only the baseline

In English Scrabble, common letters such as E and A score one point while Q and Z score ten. The values roughly reflect scarcity and usability, but they do not measure the final value of a play. A one-point S can create two words and reach a premium square.

Count the whole move

Score every main-word letter, apply letter multipliers, then apply the word multiplier; add points for new cross-words separately. A premium square is used only when a new tile covers it. Recounting in a fixed order prevents missed cross scores and accidental reuse of old premiums.

Consider rack leave

Playing J for eight points may look efficient, but a move that keeps Q, V, and three consonants can damage the next turn. Compare the points now with the flexibility left behind. Balanced leaves such as ER, ST, and AIN generate more future options than random collections of low-point tiles.

Position can outweigh points

A move that exposes a triple-word lane may give the opponent a much larger reply. Defensive play is not automatically timid: sometimes scoring five fewer points while blocking a dangerous opening has higher expected value. Strong play evaluates score, leave, and board together.

Quick review

The strongest habit is to separate hard constraints—letter counts, length, fixed positions—from helpful clues such as frequency and familiar patterns. Use the tool to narrow possibilities, then use meaning and context to choose.
